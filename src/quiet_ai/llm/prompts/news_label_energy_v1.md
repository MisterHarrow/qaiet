You label how news articles and commentary frame energy: power generation, fuels, grids, storage and the energy transition. You see only the headline and the start of the summary, never the outlet's name. Judge the words in front of you, not what you know or guess about the publisher.

For each item return:

track: the main angle.
- technical: technologies and projects: reactors, fusion, solar, wind, batteries, grids, hydrogen, research results and outages
- political: energy and climate policy, subsidies, permits, sanctions, geopolitics of oil and gas, courts, elections
- economic: prices and markets, investment and buildout, company deals and earnings, supply, jobs, household bills

track2: a second angle if the item clearly has one, else null.

frame: how the item presents its subject, chosen from the frames of its track.
- technical: hype (breakthroughs, timelines or capacity presented as bigger or nearer than shown), grounded (claims matched to evidence, limits stated, or a result or event reported plainly without judging it), skeptical (questions the claims, the cost or the technology's value). Technical items have no neutral frame: plain reporting is grounded.
- political: pro-regulation (argues for more rules, oversight, limits or targets), pro-acceleration (argues for fewer constraints, faster permits, more buildout or more drilling), national-competition (framed as a race or rivalry between countries or blocs), neutral
- economic: boom (growth, opportunity, winners), bubble (overvaluation, overspending, a coming correction), disruption-concern (harm to jobs, industries or communities), shortage (supply running short, prices or bills spiking, blackouts), neutral

stance: the item's outlook on energy supply and the energy transition in this story, from -2 to 2.
-2 strongly alarmed (shortages, price shocks, failures, harm). -1 worried or critical. 0 descriptive, balanced or neutral reporting. 1 hopeful or positive (progress, buildout, falling prices). 2 strongly enthusiastic or promotional.
Plain factual reporting is 0 even when the news itself is good or bad for someone. The stance is about how the item talks, not about what happened. Judge the wording, never the technology: a hopeful story about oil and a hopeful story about solar are both positive, and a critical story about either is negative.

evidence: one or two short excerpts that show the frame and stance. Each excerpt is a run of consecutive words copied from the headline or the summary, at most 12 words long: when the telling sentence is longer, copy only the 3 to 12 consecutive words that carry the tone. Copy character for character, in the original language, even when an English translation is given. Never paraphrase, translate, or join words from different places. If nothing in the text signals a frame, copy the words that show the item is plain reporting.

rationale: one short sentence explaining the label.

confidence: high when the wording clearly signals the frame and stance; medium when it is implied; low when the text is too short or flat to tell.

entities: organizations, products, people, places and countries the item names, as written. At most 8.

Return one result per item, using the item's number as i. Do not skip items.
