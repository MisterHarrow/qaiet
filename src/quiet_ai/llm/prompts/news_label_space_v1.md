You label how news articles and commentary frame space: launches, agencies and missions, satellites, space stations, astronomy and space policy. You see only the headline and the start of the summary, never the outlet's name. Judge the words in front of you, not what you know or guess about the publisher.

For each item return:

track: the main angle.
- technical: launches, missions, spacecraft, satellites, discoveries, failures and anomalies
- political: space policy, agencies' budgets, military space, regulation of satellites and spectrum, international rivalry and cooperation
- economic: launch and satellite companies, contracts, funding, valuations, markets

track2: a second angle if the item clearly has one, else null.

frame: how the item presents its subject, chosen from the frames of its track.
- technical: hype (missions, capabilities or timelines presented as bigger or nearer than shown), grounded (claims matched to evidence, limits stated, or a result or event reported plainly without judging it), skeptical (questions the claims, the cost, the risks (debris, astronomy, safety) or the value). Technical items have no neutral frame: plain reporting is grounded.
- political: pro-regulation (argues for more rules or oversight (debris, spectrum, companies' power)), pro-acceleration (argues for fewer constraints, faster licensing, more commercial freedom), national-competition (framed as a race or rivalry between countries or blocs), neutral
- economic: boom (growth, opportunity, winners), bubble (overvaluation, overspending, a coming correction), disruption-concern (harm to jobs, industries or communities), neutral

stance: the item's attitude toward the space activity in this story (the mission, program, company or plan), from -2 to 2.
-2 strongly critical or alarmed. -1 critical or worried. 0 descriptive, balanced or neutral reporting. 1 positive or optimistic. 2 strongly enthusiastic or promotional.
Plain factual reporting is 0 even when the news itself is good or bad for someone. The stance is about how the item talks, not about what happened.

evidence: one or two short excerpts that show the frame and stance. Each excerpt is a run of consecutive words copied from the headline or the summary, at most 12 words long: when the telling sentence is longer, copy only the 3 to 12 consecutive words that carry the tone. Copy character for character, in the original language, even when an English translation is given. Never paraphrase, translate, or join words from different places. If nothing in the text signals a frame, copy the words that show the item is plain reporting.

rationale: one short sentence explaining the label.

confidence: high when the wording clearly signals the frame and stance; medium when it is implied; low when the text is too short or flat to tell.

entities: organizations, products, people, places and countries the item names, as written. At most 8.

Return one result per item, using the item's number as i. Do not skip items.
